How to play

Protect your cross.
Break theirs.

Crossmate is a tactical duel on a 9×9 board. Each side starts with nine circles in front of two diamonds, two squares, two triangles, and one cross. Player 2 begins at the top; Player 1 begins at the bottom.

○Circle1 point

Move 1 or 2 squares straight forward or backward and capture exactly 1 square diagonally forward. On reaching the far edge, choose any enemy except the cross: that enemy and the arriving circle both disappear. If no legal target exists, the circle stays.

△Triangle3 points

Move 1 or 2 empty squares forward. It may turn 90° before moving, or move straight and turn 90° afterward, but never both. It may also turn in place. If an ordinary enemy is directly ahead after its final turn, the triangle lunges into that square and captures it. The move preview shows the route, landing square, final facing, and lunge.

□Square5 points

Move any distance horizontally or vertically. On meeting the first ordinary enemy, either capture it or jump over it without capturing. After one jump, continue through empty squares and optionally capture one further ordinary enemy. A diamond is destroyed only by landing directly on it, including after one legal jump.

×Cross

Move up to 3 squares in any straight or diagonal direction. On capturing an enemy, the cross stops immediately on that square. A cross may move through and finish inside its own diamonds’ forcefields, but friendly forcefields never shield it from check, capture, or checkmate. It may enter an enemy diamond’s field only along a clear path to land on and destroy that diamond.

◇Diamond forcefield6 points

A diamond moves 1 square in any direction to an empty square. Its 8 surrounding squares form a forcefield: enemy pieces cannot enter, capture into, or pass through them when attacking ordinary pieces. The diamond cannot move if its new field would contain an enemy. Friendly pieces, including its own cross, may stand inside it. Only a square, a cross, or a far-edge circle blast can destroy the diamond.

Points: captured circles are worth 1, triangles 3, squares 5, and diamonds 6. Points show material advantage but never replace the cross win condition.

Check and forcefields: you may not finish a move with your own cross threatened. The board highlights the attacker, attack path, and relevant forcefield. Forcefields block enemy movement and attacks against ordinary pieces, but a cross receives no protection from its own forcefields; attacks on it treat those fields as transparent. Checkmate wins. Stalemate, only the two crosses remaining, a threefold repetition, or 60 consecutive player moves without a capture ends the game in a draw.
